# US-45, big work in steps, and a week you can see coming

Step 7 of [the roadmap](../review-and-roadmap.md), the largest. Research
problem 3: long projects are ignored until the week they are due. CSE 123
Project 2b is released on 16 November and due on the 30th, and until now the
app knew about the 30th only.

This file was written as the plan before any code, as the roadmap promised,
and then updated with what building it took.

```
US-45  As someone with projects that take more than one evening,
       I want to break an item into dated steps and see how heavy each week is,
       so that big work starts when it should and a crowded week is visible
       before it arrives.

Priority: Should, built
Acceptance criteria:
  AC-45.1  Given an item, when I open it, I can add a step with a title and
           a date; the step is saved as its own deadline.
  AC-45.2  Given a step, it appears in the list and on the calendar like any
           deadline, says which item it belongs to, and inherits that item's
           course, goal and category.
  AC-45.3  Given an item with steps, its row says how many are done out of
           how many.
  AC-45.4  Given I delete an item with steps, the steps go with it, and the
           confirmation says so.
  AC-45.5  Given a version 4 database or export, it upgrades to version 5
           with no steps, and a version 5 export round trips.
  AC-45.6  Given the calendar, each week says how many open deadlines fall in
           it, and marks a heavy week.
```

## Plan

**A step is an item with a parent.** Version 5 adds `parentId` to every item,
null for an ordinary one. Because a step is an item, it already has
everything a step needs: a date, done and undo, a note, the calendar, the
phone export, overdue triage. The alternative, a list of sub-tasks stored
inside the item, would have needed every one of those rebuilt for a second
kind of thing.

**One level only.** A step cannot have steps. Its panel does not offer to add
them. Nested plans are the Notion trap the roadmap argues against.

**Steps are added from the item's panel.** Open the item, and below its
fields is a short list of its steps and a title-and-date form to add one. No
new view.

**A step inherits** the parent's course, goal and category, so the course
filter and goal progress count it without being told. Its priority starts
normal and can be changed like any item.

**Deleting the parent deletes its steps.** A step without its project has
nothing to be a step of. The confirmation names how many will go.

**Week load is a count, with a threshold.** A narrow extra column on the
calendar says "4 due" for each week; six or more is marked heavy. A count is
honest and needs no explanation, where a weighted score would need one.

**Deliberately not built:** reordering steps (they sort by date, which is the
order they happen in), a parent's date moving its steps, and progress bars.

## What it took

The plan held. Version 5 is one hop in `migrate.ts`, and the same seventeen
test fixtures that gained `repeatDay` in version 4 gained `parentId` here; the
tests that assert "the current version" as a literal number moved from 4 to 5
again, which is the cost of spelling it as a literal.

The dashboard now receives every item as well as the filtered ones, so a step's
parent and a parent's steps are found even when a filter hides one of them.

The step title field was first labelled "Step title for Midterm", which
contains "Title for Midterm", and two US-25 specs matched both. Playwright
matches labels by substring; that is the eighth collision of this kind in the
project, and the field is now "New step for Midterm".

The week column changed the count of table cells, so the test proving an empty
month still draws counts day cells by name instead of every cell.
