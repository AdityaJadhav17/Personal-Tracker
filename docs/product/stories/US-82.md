# US-82, lock a note

Asked for on 29 September 2026: lock sensitive notes so they open only with a
code Aditya sets.

```
US-82  As someone who keeps a few private things in Notes,
       I want to lock those notes behind a passcode,
       so that someone at my unlocked laptop, or holding a copy of my data,
       cannot read them.

Priority: Should
Acceptance criteria:
  AC-82.1  The open note has a Lock button. The first lock asks for a new
           passcode, twice: at least 6 characters, letters or numbers. One
           passcode locks every note.
  AC-82.2  A locked note's text is encrypted with a key made from the
           passcode. Its first line stays readable as its title; nothing
           else of it is stored or exported readable.
  AC-82.3  A locked note shows its title and a lock in the list. Opening it
           asks for the passcode; a wrong one says so and shows nothing.
  AC-82.4  After the right passcode, locked notes open and save as you type,
           encrypted, until you press Lock now, leave Notes, reload, or five
           minutes pass without typing. Then they lock again.
  AC-82.5  Remove Lock, after the passcode, makes the note ordinary again.
  AC-82.6  Search does not look inside locked notes.
  AC-82.7  Locked notes survive a reload, an export and an import, still
           encrypted. An import whose locked note or lock is malformed is
           refused. A version 7 file still opens, with nothing locked.
  AC-82.8  Before the first lock, the app says plainly that a forgotten
           passcode cannot be recovered and the locked notes would be lost.
```

## Decisions

**This revisits "no encryption at rest".** That decision said to revisit
when the app holds something Aditya would not want a person at his unlocked
laptop to read. Locked notes are that. It stays per note: nothing asks for a
passcode at launch, which was the objection to encrypting everything.

**Real encryption, not a screen.** A PIN screen over readable storage would
protect nothing: the text would sit in localStorage and in every export.
Web Crypto is built into the browser, so there is no dependency: PBKDF2 with
SHA-256 and 600,000 iterations turns the passcode into an AES-GCM key.

**Version 8.** The database gains `lock`, null until the first lock, holding
the salt and a known value encrypted under the key, so a wrong passcode is
caught even with no locked notes. A locked note's `body` holds only its
title, and `sealed` holds the encrypted text and its IV; an ordinary note's
`sealed` is null.

**A passcode, 6 or more characters of anything.** Chosen over a 4 to 6 digit
PIN: a million possible PINs can be tried offline against a copied file,
while a longer passcode with letters takes far longer. A short or common
one can still be guessed, so the passcode is only as strong as Aditya makes
it.

**The title shows.** As in Apple Notes, so a locked note can be found. The
first line is stored readable; keep secrets below it.

**No recovery.** There is no server, so nothing holds a reset. Said before
the first lock, in words.

**Merge keeps the lock here.** A file locked under a different passcode
brings notes this lock cannot open; Replace takes them whole. Recorded in the
decision log as an accepted limitation.

**Not in this story.** Changing the passcode, and Touch ID or Windows Hello.
